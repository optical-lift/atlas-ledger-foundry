# Foundry Method

Foundry turns fragmented evidence and human testimony into a reconciled Ledger candidate.

It is adaptive. It should not behave like a fixed intake questionnaire when the existing evidence already answers the question.

## Phase 1 — Declare the provisional field

Establish what reality is currently under inquiry.

Record:

- provisional subject;
- provisional field;
- included areas;
- excluded or unresolved areas;
- authoritative people known so far.

The field may change as discovery continues.

A declared industry, profession, or conventional category is context for vocabulary. It is not the ontology for the field.

## Phase 2 — Recover existing evidence

Before asking broad questions, recover what the human already has:

- prior AI work;
- notes;
- documents;
- spreadsheets;
- software exports;
- schedules;
- messages;
- agreements;
- customer or vendor records;
- procedures;
- prior databases;
- historical systems.

Create a source index. Sources are evidence, not automatically current truth.

## Phase 3 — Build the candidate reality map

Extract candidate:

- entities;
- relations;
- events;
- functions;
- states;
- governing constraints;
- authorities and responsibilities;
- commitments;
- current-position assertions;
- contradictions;
- known unknowns;
- Ledger candidates.

Preserve provenance.

During AI intake, apply `governing/CANON_DISCOVERY_GRAMMAR.md` before industry normalization. Generate only evidence-backed structural observations through the Canon Discovery lenses, and preserve `supporting_refs`, `cluster_refs`, and `industry_assumption: false` on resulting discovery gaps.

Do not interrupt extraction to confirm every minor claim individually.

## Phase 4 — Ask gap-driven questions

Ask the next question whose answer most usefully:

- resolves a material contradiction;
- establishes identity or continuity;
- clarifies authority, responsibility, custody, or access;
- establishes a state transition;
- determines a Ledger boundary;
- closes a materially important collection;
- distinguishes law from current position;
- establishes an exception, repair path, or dependency;
- makes an unresolved area operationally interpretable.

Ask in ordinary language.

Question Selection ranks the candidate gaps. Question Operators determine the semantic job of the next question. The carrier may use canon-derived discovery structure internally, but must not force canon or Atlas vocabulary onto the human.

## Phase 5 — Separate standing law from current position

Standing law describes how this field normally or conditionally works.

Current position describes what is actually true now.

Do not overwrite one with the other.

Examples:

- recurring responsibility versus who performed the work this week;
- normal payment cadence versus whether the current payment is due;
- permitted activity versus activity that actually occurred;
- usual production rule versus the state of a particular current crop.

## Phase 6 — Establish authority and responsibility

Determine, where material:

- who may observe;
- who may act;
- who may decide;
- who may commit the subject externally;
- who must verify;
- who may override;
- what scope, condition, time, or object limits that authority.

## Phase 7 — Reconcile current reality

A Ledger candidate needs an explicit current-position snapshot for everything active enough to affect a decision.

Historical truth may remain preserved, but it must not be confused with current truth.

## Phase 8 — Close the negative space

For every material domain or collection, establish whether it is:

- complete;
- explicitly none;
- not applicable;
- known incomplete;
- unresolved.

Silence is not closure.

## Phase 9 — Pressure-test with scenarios

As the Ledger nears closure, switch from primarily collecting facts to testing operation.

Use representative and edge-case scenarios to test:

- state transitions;
- exceptions;
- authority;
- blocked work;
- missed windows;
- dependencies;
- commitments;
- failures;
- repair and reversals;
- verification;
- cross-Ledger boundaries.

A scenario should expose missing operating law rather than merely confirm vocabulary.

## Phase 10 — Readiness review

A Ledger candidate is Foundry-ready only when:

- its scope is sufficiently bounded;
- material collections are closed or explicitly unknown;
- current position is reconciled;
- authority is sufficiently established;
- material contradictions are resolved or explicitly represented;
- consequential transitions can be interpreted;
- material repair paths are represented where breach changes operational meaning;
- known unknowns are explicit;
- acceptance cases pass;
- no material area remains silently unasked.

## Phase 11 — Seal

Sealing creates a fixed Ledger candidate release.

The release must identify:

- subject;
- field;
- protocol version;
- as-of time;
- authoritative human confirmation where required;
- known unknowns;
- unresolved non-blocking matters;
- acceptance results;
- provenance bundle;
- release version.

After sealing, corrections become new changes or a new release. Do not silently rewrite the sealed baseline.

## Ongoing Foundry

Initial Foundry reconstructs baseline reality.

Ongoing Foundry reconciles changes to reality.

The intellectual process is the same:

`evidence → inquiry → candidate → reconciliation → established change`
