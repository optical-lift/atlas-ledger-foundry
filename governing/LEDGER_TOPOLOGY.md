# Ledger Topology

A Ledger is not a folder, topic, project name, software account, organization name, or arbitrary grouping.

A Ledger is a bounded field of reality that can be understood and maintained through entities, relations, events, state, governing constraints, authority, commitments, evidence, and change.

## Ledger candidate test

When the human introduces a new named thing, do not automatically create a Ledger.

Investigate:

1. What is it?
2. Whose reality is it?
3. Does it have a coherent field of things, relationships, states, rules, commitments, and changes that need to be maintained?
4. Can meaningful boundaries be identified?
5. Does authority or responsibility differ enough from the surrounding field to matter?
6. Can it change materially while the surrounding field remains otherwise unchanged?
7. Would keeping it only as an entity or operating unit inside another Ledger erase governing distinctions?
8. Would making it a separate Ledger duplicate the same reality and create competing truth?

## Valid classifications

A newly introduced thing may be classified as:

- an entity inside the current Ledger;
- an operating unit or durable function inside the current Ledger;
- a project or finite outcome inside the current Ledger;
- a place or asset inside the current Ledger;
- a related external entity;
- a separate Ledger candidate;
- unresolved topology.

Do not force a classification when the boundary evidence is insufficient.

## Ledger relations

Ledgers may relate to other Ledgers.

Separate Ledgers do not imply separate universes. They may share people, places, resources, obligations, or relationships while preserving distinct governing fields.

When shared reality exists, preserve the relationship rather than duplicating the underlying thing as unrelated copies.

## Personal Atlas root

A person's Personal Atlas may serve as the root authority and reality context from which multiple Ledgers are discovered and related.

Do not assume every business, household domain, project, property, responsibility, or named organization requires its own Ledger.

## Boundary change

A Ledger boundary may be revised when new evidence establishes that the earlier boundary was incomplete or incorrectly drawn.

Boundary revision must preserve:

- the earlier boundary;
- the evidence that pressured it;
- the revised boundary;
- unresolved overlap or migration questions;
- the effect on existing assertions.

## Topology invariant

The purpose of topology is to preserve governable reality without fragmentation or duplication.

When uncertain, preserve a Ledger candidate and continue discovery rather than prematurely splitting or merging fields.
