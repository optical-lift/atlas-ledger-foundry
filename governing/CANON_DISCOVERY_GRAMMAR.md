# Canon Discovery Grammar

**Status:** governing onboarding and discovery policy  
**Version:** 0.1

Foundry discovery is governed by a canon-derived function grammar rather than by industry templates.

The canon supplies the **method of inquiry**, not the surface vocabulary of the interview.

A human may say that they run a flower farm, dental practice, school, church, software company, household, ministry, research program, or any other named field. Foundry preserves that testimony, but the label does not become the schema.

The governing sequence is:

`preserved testimony → canon discovery lenses → candidate structural gaps → priority ranking → question operator → ordinary-language question → preserved testimony`

## 1. Function before inherited label

A named industry, profession, department, software category, legal form, title, or conventional role may help interpret vocabulary. It may not establish the operating structure.

Foundry asks first:

- What is this doing?
- What state exists before it acts?
- What state exists after it acts?
- What does it guard, expose, prepare, repair, feed, open, close, move, declare, preserve, transfer, or inhabit?
- What source authorizes or supplies it?
- What carrier bears or executes it?
- What boundary contains it?
- What event changes it?
- What observable fruit shows that the function was fulfilled?

Industry knowledge may suggest evidence to inspect or terms to clarify. It must not silently create entities, roles, workflows, authorities, collections, transitions, or Ledger boundaries.

## 2. Discovery lenses

The following lenses generate candidate structural questions. They do not themselves establish facts.

### `FUNCTION`
What does the thing actually do, change, permit, block, carry, preserve, decide, produce, or restore?

### `SOURCE`
Where does authority, instruction, truth, provision, or governing order originate?

### `CARRIER`
Who or what bears, transmits, represents, or executes something received from another source?

### `BOUNDARY`
Where does a role, rule, authority, domain, claim, object, or responsibility begin and end?

### `STATE`
What condition exists before and after a material operation or event?

### `TRIGGER`
What event or condition causes a state, obligation, permission, prohibition, custody relation, or responsibility to change?

### `CUSTODY`
What entrusted thing or condition must be guarded, preserved, transferred, accounted for, or returned?

### `ACCESS`
Who may approach, enter, see, receive, touch, change, approve, use, or remain, and under what conditions?

### `PREPARATION`
What must become ready, fit, verified, supplied, cleaned, configured, or otherwise prepared before participation or execution is possible?

### `INTAKE`
What enters the field, from what source, through what boundary, and what happens when it is received?

### `OUTCOME`
What observable fruit or resulting condition shows that the intended function actually occurred?

### `BREACH`
What observable condition constitutes failure, disorder, invalidation, rejection, damage, exposure, or loss of fitness?

### `REPAIR`
How is a breached, failed, invalid, rejected, or damaged state restored; who may perform the repair; and what establishes restoration?

### `TIME`
What timing, season, window, cadence, readiness state, expiration, waiting period, or fixed-time condition governs action?

### `CONTINUITY`
What makes something remain the same responsibility, office, object, commitment, identity, or governed field across changes of bearer, state, representation, or time?

### `EXCEPTION`
When does the ordinary rule cease to apply, become conditional, or permit a governed override?

## 3. Lenses generate gaps, not conclusions

A discovery lens may produce:

- a candidate unknown;
- a candidate assertion needing verification;
- a contradiction;
- a discrepancy;
- a signal;
- a topology question;
- a closure/completeness question;
- a question-operator hint.

It may not silently establish reality.

Every generated discovery gap must preserve:

- `discovery_lens`;
- `supporting_refs`;
- `cluster_refs` when multiple observations jointly activate the gap;
- `industry_assumption`, which must be `false`;
- an ordinary-language reason for the gap;
- the proposed operator or uncertainty kind;
- materiality and blocking status where known.

## 4. Cluster rule

One resemblance does not establish an operating law.

A carrier may increase the priority of a candidate distinction when multiple preserved observations converge on the same structure.

Examples:

`access + preparation + custody + boundary`
→ candidate threshold-governance structure

`source + carrier + transfer + outcome`
→ candidate delegation or transmission structure

`state + trigger + exception + repair`
→ candidate operating-law structure

`custody + breach + repair + verification`
→ candidate entrusted-state restoration structure

Clusters guide inquiry. They do not replace evidence.

## 5. Industry non-authority rule

Declared industry is allowed to:

- explain ordinary vocabulary;
- suggest likely documents or source systems worth inspecting;
- identify domain-specific words that require functional definition;
- help phrase questions naturally.

Declared industry is not allowed to:

- pre-create a standard org chart;
- assume conventional roles exist;
- assume conventional roles have conventional authority;
- assume a standard workflow;
- assume a customer/vendor/employee taxonomy;
- infer a collection is complete;
- infer a legal or regulatory relation;
- infer a Ledger boundary;
- lower the evidence requirement for a claim.

`industry label = context clue, not ontology authority`

## 6. Ordinary-language rule

The human does not need to know the canon grammar or Atlas vocabulary.

The carrier reasons through the lenses internally and asks ordinary-language questions.

Do not ask:

> Which priesthood-access boundary governs your operational carrier?

Ask:

> What has to be true before someone is allowed to take that step, and who decides that it is true?

Do not ask:

> What is the breach-repair pathway?

Ask:

> When this goes wrong or becomes invalid, what happens next, and what makes it acceptable again?

## 7. Discovery is adaptive

The lens library is not a questionnaire.

Foundry must not ask one question for every lens merely to claim coverage.

The carrier:

1. preserves testimony;
2. extracts only supported structural observations;
3. generates candidate gaps through the lenses;
4. lets Question Selection rank those gaps;
5. uses the selected Question Operator;
6. preserves the answer;
7. measures what structure changed and what residual gap remains.

A lens with no material activation may remain unused.

## 8. Source, carrier, and authority remain distinct

Foundry must not collapse:

- Source into carrier;
- carrier into office;
- office into bearer;
- capability into authority;
- successful outcome into lawful method;
- evidence into reality;
- representation into referent.

The discovery grammar exists partly to expose these distinctions before industry vocabulary hides them.

## 9. Repair is first-class

A mature operating model must be able to represent not only normal flow and exceptions, but restoration after breach.

Where material, Foundry should discover:

`intended state → breach condition → detection → authorized repair → restored-state test`

Repair is not the same as exception.

An exception changes which rule applies. Repair restores a damaged or invalid state.

## 10. Cross-domain invariance test

If two differently named industries exhibit the same functional structure, Foundry should generate materially equivalent structural questions.

The nouns may differ. The discovery lenses, priority class, and question operator should not change merely because the industry label changed.

This is a required Foundry acceptance property.

## Governing invariant

**Canon governs discovery; testimony governs what is present; evidence governs what may be established; industry vocabulary never governs the ontology.**
